Specialist streams · Plastercycle

Plasterboard in. Gypsum out.

Plastercycle sets an enclosed, weatherproof bin down at a transfer station, depot or building site. Plasterboard goes in through the side and stays dry. When it's full, our hook-lift trucks take it away to be recycled back into gypsum instead of landfill.

How it works

Four steps. Nothing gets wet.

Plasterboard is only recyclable while it's clean, separate and dry. The bin is built to keep it that way from the site to the recycler.

01

We drop the bin

Our hook-lift truck sets the 6.3 m bin down at your transfer station, depot or building site.

02

You fill it through the side

Lift a top-hinged panel and slide or throw the sheets in. The opening starts about a metre off the ground, so no one climbs in.

03

It stays dry

A closed roof, solid rear door and shut panels keep rain off the load. Wet board is heavier to haul and harder to recycle.

04

We take it to be recycled

When it's full, the same trucks collect it. Smooth inside walls let the load tip out cleanly, and the board is processed back into gypsum and paper.

The landfill levy

Every tonne buried is a tonne of levy.

Who it's for

Put a bin where the plasterboard turns up.

The same bin works at a gate, on a construction site, on a house block or at a trade counter. We set it down and collect it on our own trucks.

Councils and transfer stations

A bin by the gate. Plasterboard leaves as a recyclable, not as levy.

ResultLess levy paid

Builders and construction sites

Offcuts into the bin as you go. One line in the waste plan, sorted.

ResultA cleaner site

Residential builders

A bin on the house site while the plasterers work. We collect it when it's full.

ResultNo skip full of board

Plaster manufacturers and retailers

Plasterers bring back their offcuts when they collect their next load.

ResultBoard back to the source

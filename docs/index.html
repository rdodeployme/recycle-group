Where it started

The mattress problem.

Victoria was landfilling around 160,000 mattresses a year. They are bulky, they don't compact, and once they are buried the steel, foam, timber and fibre inside them are lost for good.

The scale of itA year of mattresses laid flat across the MCG playing surface: about 26 deep, over 6 m high. Between 15 and 36 deep, depending on mattress size.

How we solved it

We built a plant to take every mattress apart.

  1. 01CollectMobile mattress recycling points on council sites and our transfer stations bring them in, without double handling.
  2. 02ShredAbout 30 seconds per mattress. A 600 hp twin-shaft shredder handles up to 1,500 m³ of product a day.
  3. 03SeparateMagnets, vibration, suction and an electrostatic fibre separator pull the spring steel out 99% free of fabric.
  4. 04Return to marketSpring steel is baled and exported as a commodity. Mattress pulp is in testing as noise walls and erosion control fabric.

Then came the foam

The polystyrene problem.

A regional Victorian transfer station opened in 2025 and was quickly overwhelmed by polystyrene from domestic customers. On some days more than 150 m³ of it came through the gate.

How we solved it

We built Australia's largest polystyrene facility.

  1. 01CapacityUp to 500 m³ of polystyrene a day at full capacity.
  2. 02DensifyHot-melt densifiers and a cold press turn loose foam into solid blocks.
  3. 03ShrinkThree shipping containers of foam become one cube the size of a washing machine.
  4. 04ReuseThe result is a 100% recyclable and reusable product, exported under the group's own licence.

Then the roadsides

The illegal dumping problem.

Within 30 km of a regional Victorian transfer station, illegal roadside dumping had reached epidemic proportions. The challenge was simple to state and hard to solve: reduce it.

How we solved it

We made doing the right thing the easier option.

  1. 01Cost-effective tippingThe only way to reduce dumping was to give customers a cost-effective alternative to it.
  2. 0290 streamsMore than 90 waste streams separated under cover, so almost everything has somewhere to go.
  3. 0310 of them freeTen of those streams cost nothing to drop off.
  4. 04Recycled at volume96% of everything arriving is recycled. Within the first four months, the effect on illegal dumping was profound.

Gas canisters · In development

Nitrous oxide canisters

Disposable nitrous oxide cylinders are turning up in kerbside bins, transfer stations and dumped rubbish in growing numbers. They often still hold gas, so they can't go into a compactor, shredder or baler as they are, and they can't be recycled as metal until they are empty.

We are designing a six-cylinder decommissioning machine: cylinders are pierced behind an interlocked steel guard, the residual gas is drawn off, and the empty cylinders go to metal recycling.

Why we innovate

Australia can't burn its way out of waste.

China and much of Europe send huge volumes of waste to incinerators for energy. In Australia, waste-to-energy is tightly restricted, so the answer has to be recycling, from the mainstream to the complex. That is what Recycle Group builds.

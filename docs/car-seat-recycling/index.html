Service · Child car seat recycling

Expired seats. Taken apart.

Child car seats expire. Once a seat is past its date, or has been in a crash, it shouldn't carry a child again, and most end up in landfill. We collect them, cut the harness so no seat goes back on the road, take each one apart by hand and send the steel, plastic and foam into recycling.

How it works

Cut, stripped, sorted, recycled.

A car seat is several materials fixed together, which is why most go straight to landfill. Taken apart by hand, more than 80% of it can be recycled.

01

We set up a collection point

A marked cage or bay at your transfer station, store or depot. Or we collect seats in bulk from wherever they've built up.

02

The harness is cut first

Every seat has its straps cut on arrival, so it can never go back into a car. Then it's taken apart by hand.

03

Sorted by material

Steel, plastic shell, impact foam and fabric go into separate streams. Clean streams are what make recycling work.

04

Recycled and reported

Each stream goes into the group's metal, plastic and polystyrene recycling. Every collection is weighed and reported back to you.

The problem

Every seat has an expiry date.

Plastic and foam weaken with age and use, so a car seat has a working life of about ten years. After that, or after a crash, it shouldn't be used again. It can't be sold on, and charities are often left paying to get rid of it.

200k
Car seats to landfill every year in Australia

More than a million new seats, capsules and boosters are sold in Australia every year. At about 5 kg a seat, the 200,000 thrown out each year weigh about a thousand tonnes.

Councils · Melbourne · New program

Polystyrene densified at the council's own transfer station.

A metropolitan Melbourne council

The problem

Expanded polystyrene is one of the hardest streams a transfer station handles. It weighs almost nothing, fills a bin in hours and costs a fortune to move loose. Most of what you pay to transport is air.

What we are doing

  • Installing a hot-melt densifier at the transfer station, with delivery, commissioning and training for council staff
  • The unit shreds clean foam, melts it at around 220°C and presses it into solid ingots, stored in IBCs we supply free and collect when full
  • Every ingot goes through our recycling network and comes back as picture frames, skirting boards, insulation, outdoor furniture and packaging
90:1volume reduction, on siteBefore a truck is ever called
~90m³of loose foam in one IBC of ingotsAbout thirty 3 m³ bags, or a semi-trailer load

Volume reduction is the hot-melt unit's specification (up to 90:1); one IBC holds about 1 m³ of ingots. It is the same hot-melt process we run at our North Geelong plant. Collected volumes and tonnes will be reported once collections begin.

Councils: how we work
Clearance of redundant port infrastructure
Ports · Victoria · September 2026

A port precinct cleared, and 327 tonnes sent back into use.

A major Victorian port operator

The brief

Redundant port infrastructure had built up in one yard: rubber-coated steel bollards, timber steps and pylons, electrical boards, concrete footings, machinery and a retired aluminium boat. It all had to be cleared, and the outcome reported.

What we did

  • Cleared 167 m³ of material in fourteen item groups and brought it to our own EPA-registered facility
  • Took mixed items apart: rubber off the steel bollards, boards dismantled to AS/NZS 5377, fixings out of the timber
  • Sent six material streams to the processor that turns each back into a raw material: steel and aluminium to remelting, concrete crushed to aggregate
  • Delivered an item-by-item recovery report with tonnes, method and environmental outcomes, ready for the port's sustainability reporting
327trecovered, not landfilledThe mass of more than ten fully loaded 40-foot shipping containers
65%returned to metal productionSteel and aluminium sent to remelting
171tCO₂-e avoidedThe emissions of 55 Victorian homes' electricity for a year

Volumes recorded on site and converted to tonnes with EPA Victoria's published factors.

Councils · Victoria · FY2025–26

A council's mattresses and couches, recovered across two transfer stations.

A Victorian coastal shire council

The brief

The council needed mattresses and couches dropped at two of its transfer stations taken out of the landfill stream, with the outcome reported back for the year.

What we did

  • Took mattresses and couches from both transfer stations through the year
  • Stripped every item at The Mattress Recycling Company: spring steel and timber recovered, the residual shredded
  • Issued a Recycling Outcomes Report for the financial year, broken out by site and by item size

890tof steel recoveredAbout 29 fully loaded 40-foot shipping containers
1,600tCO₂-e avoidedFrom the recovered steel alone

Mattress count supplied by Recycle Group. Steel estimated at about 27 kg per mattress (≈270 t per 10,000 at The Mattress Recycling Company); carbon at 1.79 t CO₂-e per tonne of steel recovered.

Tunnel-project packaging, recovered
Tunnel & infrastructure construction

Every piece of polystyrene from a tunnel project, recycled.

A major tunnel construction project

The brief

The project's equipment arrived packed in expanded polystyrene. Loose EPS is almost all air: it fills bins in an hour, blows around a site and takes up landfill out of all proportion to its weight.

What we did

  • Built a way to recover all of the project's polystyrene packaging rather than skip it
  • Densified it at Australia's fastest-growing polystyrene plant
  • Turned it into dense polymer that goes back into manufacturing
100%of the project's polystyrene recycledNone of it sent to landfill
300+m³of loose EPS densified a dayThree shipping containers of foam into one washing-machine-sized cube

Plant capacity figures are Recycle Group operating figures. Project name and volumes on request.

Gas canisters · In development

Nitrous oxide cylinders, emptied safely and recycled as metal.

Recycle Group's own decommissioning line

The problem

Disposable nitrous oxide cylinders are turning up in kerbside bins, transfer stations and dumped rubbish in growing numbers. They often still hold gas, so they can't go into a compactor, shredder or baler as they are, and they can't be recycled as metal until they are empty.

What we are building

  • Designing a six-cylinder decommissioning machine: cylinders are loaded into cradles and held in place behind an interlocked steel guard
  • A multi-station hydraulic ram pierces all six at once, and the residual gas is drawn off through engineered extraction to ventilation or gas treatment
  • Pierced cylinders drop to a discharge cage and go to metal recycling, steel and aluminium separated
  • Emergency stop, guarded loading and a machine that only runs with the doors closed
6cylinders decommissioned per cycleFour, five or six stations, depending on the build
4steps: load, pierce, extract, recycleEvery cylinder emptied before it reaches a shredder or baler

Concept design shown. The machine is in development and the final specification may change.

Councils: how we work
Spring steel to export
Export · trans-global

Mattress spring steel, baled and exported as a commodity.

International steel buyers

The brief

Around a third of a mattress by weight is steel. Victoria was landfilling 160,000 mattresses a year, and the steel went with them.

What we did

  • Process about 13,000 mattresses a month at The Mattress Recycling Company
  • Shred and magnetically separate the spring steel to about 99% ferrous
  • Bale it on our own baler and load containers on site for direct export

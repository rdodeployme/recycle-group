The Mattress Recycling Company

Mattresses

Victoria was landfilling 160,000 mattresses a year. The Mattress Recycling Company recovers them at a 99% recovery rate: every mattress is stripped to steel, foam, timber and fibre. The steel is baled and exported as a commodity; per 10,000 mattresses around 270 tonnes of steel come out of 800 tonnes in, with 99% of that steel recovered. Shredded residual is a quarter of its original volume, meaning less landfill space and better compaction.

Styro

Polystyrene (EPS)

Expanded polystyrene is up to 98% air. Styro densifies it into blocks a fraction of the size and reprocesses it for products such as picture frames, cornices and laminated timber, and exports processed EPS under the group's own licence. Australia generates around 45,000 tonnes of polystyrene a year.

Inside a mattress

One mattress. Four materials. About 38 seconds.

Every mattress that reaches The Mattress Recycling Company is taken apart, not buried. Scroll to pull one apart.

01 · Fabric and ticking

The cover comes off first.

Fabric and ticking are cut away from the spring unit. What can't yet be recycled is shredded to a quarter of its volume and is the subject of live trials, including noise walls and erosion control fabric.

02 · Foam and fibre

Foam and fibre, separated.

Comfort foam and fibre pads are pulled from the springs and sent down their own pathways, so they don't contaminate the steel.

03 · Spring steel

A third of the mattress is steel. We recover 99% of it.

The spring unit is shredded and magnetically separated to about 99% ferrous, baled on our own baler and exported as a commodity.

04 · Timber

The base gives up its timber.

Timber frames from bases are separated from the steel and fabric and recovered for reprocessing.

Our approach

Bring us the problem.

We don't simply want organisations to send us their waste. We want them to bring us their recycling problems. Recycle Group is Australian owned, with a history in recycling and construction dating back to 1957.

Start with the problem, not the bin

We start by understanding the material.

We don't arrive with a predetermined waste service and make the problem fit it. Some streams already have established recycling pathways. Others are difficult, emerging, geographically isolated, contaminated, bulky, or have never had an economically or operationally practical solution. Those are where we want to be involved.

The same philosophy, applied to very different problems.

Every one of these started as a material with no good answer. Each one shaped how Recycle Group works today.

The problemMattresses were being buried in landfill.

What we didWe invested in large-scale mattress recycling and material recovery.See more

02PolystyreneIn operation

The problemEnormous volumes of polystyrene had become a problem.

What we didWe invested in specialised hot-melt and compaction technology.See more

03Tunnel polystyreneDelivered

The problemConstruction polystyrene from a major Melbourne infrastructure project needed an alternative to landfill.

What we didWe developed a pathway capable of processing the material.See more

How a complex program starts

Pilot. Measure. Improve. Expand.

A complex recycling challenge doesn't need to begin with a massive program. It can begin with a pilot, and that creates measurable outcomes rather than promises.

01Pilot.Identify one material or one location.
02Measure.Establish the collection and processing pathway and measure the results.
03Improve.Understand what worked and improve the process.
04Expand.Develop a roadmap for expansion.

Over time, our reporting shows the quantities collected, the materials recovered, landfill diversion, reuse outcomes and where the program can improve next. How we report

Our philosophy

Landfill moves a problem to another place, another time, another generation. Recycling asks a different question: what can this become?

  • A reusable product.
  • A recovered raw material.
  • A community resource.
  • A new manufacturing input.
  • The beginning of an entirely new recycling solution.
